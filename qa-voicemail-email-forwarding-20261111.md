## Delivery limits and live checks

Twilio documents `RecordingSid` on `<Record>` recording status callbacks. It also documents a 15-second upper bound on voice HTTP requests and a default connection-override retry policy of `ct`, not `5xx` ([callback fields](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/record), [retry policy](https://www.twilio.com/docs/usage/webhooks/webhooks-connection-overrides)). The callback now acknowledges completed recordings only after voicemail and eligible email work commit. Audio analysis still occurs before that response, so real callback duration and retry configuration need provider verification. Mail runs asynchronously from persisted work, with an atomic lease and at most five attempts. Provider acceptance does not prove delivery into the recipient inbox.

The outbox row is unique per Twilio recording SID and the worker checks the current account preference before each attempt. A provider submission that succeeds just before the process stops or the database success transaction fails may be submitted again after the lease expires. This is at-least-once delivery, not exactly once; provider idempotency support would be required to close that ambiguity. An in-flight provider request lasting beyond the five-minute lease can likewise overlap a recovered attempt. After five unsuccessful attempts, or an expired fifth claim with unknown outcome, work remains visibly `FAILED` for operator review rather than silently disappearing. Older voicemail rows created before deterministic SID IDs are not backfilled into this outbox.

Before release, verify with real Twilio and an owned test mailbox: recording callback signature and timing; enabled/disabled delivery; delayed transcription; duplicate callback behavior; mail provider rejection and retry policy; authenticated playback after the email link; and account isolation.
